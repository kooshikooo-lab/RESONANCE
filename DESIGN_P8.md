# RESONANCE - DESIGN_P8: Technology - the shapes of astonishment

> "They didn't build machines that grow, or sing, or dream. They built machines
> that listen - because their whole civilization was already one long conversation."

Tone: **warm, light, often funny; serious only when it needs to be.** This is the
technology bible. Its rule: **no magic, no "quantum" shrugs - every technology in
the universe is a 500-year extrapolation of something real being built today**, and
its power should feel *astonishing but explicable*. The player should be able to
say "of course" the moment a technology is explained - and still feel small.

This document is grounded in live research (2024-2026):

## 1. The rule of astonishment

Every technology must do three things:
1. **Impress** - it must look impossible to the player.
2. **Make sense** - on hearing it, the player thinks "of course, that's just X
   pushed to its limit."
3. **Cost something** - no technology in this game is free. Astonishment has a
   price, and the price is what the story runs on.

The deepest of the three: **technology is culture.** A species' technology is not
a toolbox - it is their *inner life made physical*. You can tell what a people
love, fear, and believe by looking at what they know how to build. The game's
two civilizations - the Aria's humans and the Havari - are *proofs of this*: show
the player their tech, and the player understands their souls before a word is
spoken.

### 1.5 The two sins of futurism (and how this universe refuses both)

Most science fiction makes the same two mistakes, and this bible forbids them:

- **Sin one - underestimating everything.** A five-hundred-year future shown as
  *today with nicer gadgets* is not a future at all. **Solving aging in fifty
  years is doable.** Five hundred years is the distance from a galley to a fusion
  reactor, from a quill to a genome. The honest question is not "what if today,
  but more" - it is "what does a civilization look like when the body, the cell,
  and the molecule are fully programmable?" If a 2180 human is recognizable to a
  2025 human, the design has failed.
- **Sin two - overestimating the impossible.** The one thing that does *not*
  compound is not engineering - it is **physics**. Faster-than-light travel is not
  hard, it is (as far as anyone has ever measured) *forbidden*: it would demand
  infinite energy, or travel back in time, or negative mass we have never found.
  **A warp drive in fifty years is not happening - in five hundred years it still
  is not happening.** There are no warp drives in this universe and never will be,
  and that wall is the whole point. The Aria takes ninety years to cross 4.2 light-
  years at barely five percent of lightspeed - the true, dull, breathtaking
  reality of the nearest star. The Havari are not travelers at all; the Pulse is
  Proxima's own voice, and they have always been here. **The entire universe is the
  story of the two nearest star systems, twenty-five trillion miles apart, and
  everything of meaning in it happens inside that one thin neighborhood.** No
  teleporters, no wormholes, no hyperdrive. The distances are real, and the
  distances are why the stories are slow and warm instead of loud.

The rule that follows: **everything can compound without bound except the speed of
light - and every society in this universe is defined by how it lives against that
one wall.** The Havari did it by never leaving home. The Aria did it by *leaving*,
in the one slow way the universe permits.

## 2. The Aria's humans - technology that is *earned, not grown*

Earth tech is brilliant, careful, and a little scared of itself. It is technology
of *separation*: machines that keep their distance from the body, instruments that
insulate. Humans learned the hard way (the wars, the long silence) and their
engineering has a wound in it: **every human device can be turned off, and every
human device keeps a record.**

### 2.1 The Aria itself - a ship that is a *contract*
- **Drive:** staged D-He3 fusion, ~5% c. The drive is a *generation-long engine* -
  its maintenance schedule is a heritage ritual; children inherit "their" reactor
  component the way Earth kids inherited names.
- **The hull is a garden:** everything is recyclable, everything is calibrated to
  last four generations. Nothing on the Aria is disposable - because out here,
  nothing can be.
- **The Log:** the ship keeps a complete, tamper-evident record of everything that
  ever happened on board. Voss respects the Log the way a priest respects a
  scripture. It is *honest*, and honesty is the Aria's religion.

### 2.2 Human medicine - repair as procedure
- **Cryo-to-palliative medicine:** not the "cure everything" of the aliens, but a
  deep, careful, *surgical* medicine. Human medtech is brilliant and conservative.
- **The BCI kit:** humans *have* brain-computer interfaces - fine, precise ones,
  descended from today's 65k-electrode chips and neural speech decoders. But they
  are **wearables, not implants**: headbands and throat-collars worn like
  spectacles, removable, audited, always with a physical kill-switch. Humans
  refused to let their minds be fused with anything they couldn't take off.
  (This is the human's tell: *fear of being unable to stop.*)
- **Translation software:** the Speaker's toolkit - real-time pitch contour
  analysis, phrase matching, the "ribbon" visualization. Human tech *translates
  the aliens* by drawing their meaning as a curve. It is a beautiful instrument
  and it is exactly wrong where it matters, because it treats meaning as data
  instead of as an offering. (See DESIGN_P3: translation is a moral act.)

### 2.3 What humans cannot do (and why it matters)
Humans cannot:
- change their shape (they can repair it, painfully, never *shape* it)
- speak mind-to-mind (they can decode, never converse)
- grow a tool (they must build it, and it stays built)
- un-remember anything the Log records (their past is a prison of accuracy)

Every one of these *inabilities* is a plot-shape waiting to be used. Voss's arc,
the Speaker's arc, and the theme of imperfection all *run on the fact that human
tech cannot make a human perfect.*

## 3. The Havari - technology that is ordinary to them (and that is the astonishment)

> The Havari did not invent machines that grow, or sing, or dream. They invented
> machines the way anyone invents machines - to get a job done. The strangeness is
> not in the machines. The strangeness is in *what the machines are for*: every one
> of them exists to serve a people who speak with their whole self.

The Havari are not mystics, and their technology is not magic. It is the honest
engineering of a civilization that stayed home (§1.5) and had a very long time to get
things *right*. The thing a human notices is not that a Havari machine is beyond
understanding - it is that it is *thoroughly understandable* - and yet it was built
around a sense of "useful" that no human would have thought of first.

### 3.1 Sound is their interface (their "internet" is a conversation)
- The Havari almost never wrote. So their machines almost never print. A Havari's
  tools **speak and listen**: instruments that hum their state, doors that answer
  when you call to them, archives you ask rather than browse. Their "screens" are
  rooms you walk into and are *told* things in.
- Long-distance Havari communication is **broadcast voice** - the radio Earth had
  for a century before it stopped bothering - except nobody ever stopped, because
  to a voice-people, a signal that carries feeling is *information*. A weather
  report is sung. A warning is a tritone.
- The astonishment for humans is not the technology. It is that **there is no text
  anywhere.** A Havari's record of a treaty is a person who memorized it - the
  treaty exists as long as someone can sing it. (This is the tech that makes the
  no-lies rule *real*: when your records are voice, and your voice carries your
  feeling, a dishonest document is literally impossible to make. Lying is not
  forbidden among the Havari. It is simply *audible*.)

### 3.2 Medicine - the one machine that cannot cheat
- Havari medicine is superb: they have solved most of what a body can go wrong
  with, the way a very old civilization does - carefully, over a long time, with
  excellent instruments and no shortcuts. They have no "cure everything"; they
  have *reliable repair*.
- The one thing their medicine cannot do is **give back a voice that is truly
  gone** - and for a people whose self is their voice, that is the only loss that
  counts. This is not a limit of cleverness; it is a limit of honesty. A lost
  voice cannot be faked back into existence without the person who lost it.
  (This is the tech that makes Voss's and the Speaker's stories *rhyme with the
  Havari*: a broken voice is not a flaw in their engineering - it is a wound that
  medicine, human or Havari, declines to patch.)

### 3.3 What astonishes humans is what the Havari *don't* have
A human engineer walking through Havari workspaces is struck by the absences:
- **No text.** No books, no signs, no print. (Their library is a choir - but a
  choir is just people doing a job, and it is the most normal thing in their world.)
- **No acoustic privacy.** Their rooms are not walls so much as *thinning*; sound
  passes, and they have never needed soundproofing, because nobody in their
  society says anything they need hidden. (To a human this feels like being in a
  glass room. To a Havari, a human's soundproof rooms feel like being shouted at
  through a pillow.)
- **No "off" for the voice.** A Havari tool does not go silent when unused; it
  keeps a low idle hum, like a cat. A Havari is never in total silence, and they
  find human silence *distressing*, the way a human finds a room that has been
  too quiet for too long.

The point is not that Havari technology is alive. It is that **their technology is
social.** Every machine is built the way a good conversationalist builds a remark:
to be heard, and to invite a reply.

## 4. Other species at the outpost (a few notes)

The outpost is crowded (DESIGN_P7). Other crews have their own engineering and their
own culture. Three sketched so far - each is *one sentence of ordinary astonishment*:

- **The Vessk (radial, chitin-shelled):** their technology is *durable memory*. They
  record everything by engraving the shells they shed, so a Vessk archive is a
  mountain of physical records older than any living Vessk. They keep what matters,
  physically, forever.
  (Astonishment: *their oldest records outdate their own memory of building them -
  they have files whose purpose no one remembers, and they keep them anyway.*)
- **The Umlou (slow, soft, gravid):** their technology is *patience*. They build
  slowly, meticulously, and their machines are designed to outlive their own need for
  maintenance. An Umlou device is a small, perfect, boring object that never breaks.
  (Astonishment: *their tools have no instructions, because nothing they build ever
  needs fixing.*)
- **The Chirr (small, fast, many-limbed):** their technology is *borrowed*. They
  arrived light and have spent the season repurposing the outpost's own infrastructure
  for their own ends, to everyone's mixed delight and alarm. They are the outpost's
  unruly younger siblings and its best practical engineers.
  (Astonishment: *they can make your own lights do things the manual says they cannot
  - and they will, whether you asked or not.*)

These are seeds, not commitments. Each can grow into a friend, a foil, or a background
laugh as the stories need.

## 5. The two-culture meeting (a theme for the universe, not a locked plot)

The whole universe's recurring warmth is *two ways of being, neither one wrong*,
rubbing against each other:

| The Aria's humans | The Havari |
|---|---|
| Tech is **built**, separate from the body, auditable, kill-switchable | Tech is **made**, ordinary to them, built around voice rather than text |
| Medicine **repairs**; it does not remake | Medicine **repairs**; even it cannot give back a lost voice |
| Minds are **private**; you broadcast a filtered self | Voices are **transparent**; your feeling rides in every word |
| Honesty is a **choice** (the Log records what you did) | Honesty is **audible** (the tone carries who you are) |
| Perfection is **impossible** (and they grieve it) | Perfection is **practiced** (and it can smother them) |
| The past is a **document** (accurate, fixed) | The past is a **song** (performed, alive) |

Neither side is right. This is not "humans win by being imperfect." It is a theme the
universe keeps returning to: **individuals shaped by one way of being, meeting
individuals shaped by another, and each quietly learning the use of the thing they
lack.** Voss learns to be heard. A Havari learns to be imperfect on purpose. In a
utopian universe these meetings are warm and often funny - serious only when a story
needs them to be.

And - the quietest, warmest truth of all: **a voice is the one thing both species
already share, and neither one has finished growing.**

## 6. Open questions (research + design)

1. **How much does the player know at start?** The Speaker is a human linguist - do
   they understand the Havari's voice-transparency (that their tone carries feeling,
   and so lying is *audible*) at the beginning, or learn it alongside the player?
   (Design lean: they know the *linguistics* but not the *lived cost* - the reveal
   lands on them too.)
2. **Voss and the BCI kit:** does Voss wear the human headband-and-collar BCI? A nice
   beat: Voss, who "can't sing," is the one human most *wired* to machines - a quiet
   symmetry with the Havari he fears.
3. **The Log's limit:** if the Log records everything, what did it *fail* to record
   that matters? (The one honest gap that saves a life, or damns one.)
4. **Other species' tech:** which of the three sketched species earns a *scene*, and
   which stays background color? (Lean: the Chirr get a scene - they're fun, and they
   can be the outpost's first to repurpose a Havari-built system for their own ends,
   which is a delightful near-miss.)
5. **Should the no-lies rule be visible as an *effect* in-game** (a subtle pitch-trace
   or shimmer when a Havari speaker's tone wavers) as the visual proof of the bible?
