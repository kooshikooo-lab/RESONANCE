# RESONANCE - DESIGN_P8: Technology - the shapes of astonishment

> "They didn't build their machines. They *grew* them, the way a tree grows
> bark, and the bark is still listening."

Tone: **warm, light, often funny; serious only when it needs to be.** This is the
technology bible. Its rule: **no magic, no "quantum" shrugs - every technology in
the game is a 500-year extrapolation of something real being built today**, and
its power should feel *astonishing but explicable*. The player should be able to
say "of course" the moment a technology is explained - and still feel small.

This document is grounded in live research (2024-2026):

| Real seed | Where it lives today | 500 years later |
|---|---|---|
| Deblina Sarkar's cell-electronics hybrids ("Circulatronics"), subcellular wireless nano-antennas inside cells, 6-atom transistors, surgery-free self-implanting brain devices | MIT Nano-Cybernetic Biotrek | fully fused nano-biology: machines that are *part of* living tissue, invisible, self-repairing, seamless |
| Michael Levin's bioelectric code: ion-channel/gap-junction networks that compute *morphology*; rewriting the "target shape" a body regenerates toward; planaria 2-head/0-head reprogramming; eyes induced anywhere | Tufts Allen Discovery Center | total control of growth and form - the *anatomical compiler* |
| Xenobots / anthrobots / neurobots: living machines from wild-type cells, self-repairing, sound-responsive, kinematic self-replication | Tufts / Wyss / UVM | biodegradable living technology - tools that are also organisms |
| Programmable matter / atomically precise manufacturing (silicon dangling-bond logic, DNA-origami superlattices, self-reconfigurable voxel robots) | MIT CBA, ACS, Nature Comms | smart matter: objects that re-shape, re-color, re-purpose on command |
| Bidirectional BCI: brain-to-brain interface (EEG->TMS, 2013-14), 65,536-electrode subdural chip, speech + *singing* via neural decoding, human-avatar tactile sharing | PLOS/Stanford/UCSF/Nature Electronics | technological telepathy, fused with the body, natural as a handshake |
| Medical nanorobots: micro-surgeons, targeted delivery, cell therapy, partial cell reprogramming / rejuvenation (Yamanaka factors) | Nature, ACS, RSC | the end of disease as we know it - body repair as routine infrastructure |

## 1. The rule of astonishment

Every technology must do three things:
1. **Impress** - it must look impossible to the player.
2. **Make sense** - on hearing it, the player thinks "of course, that's just X
   pushed to its limit."
3. **Cost something** - no technology in this game is free. Astonishment has a
   price, and the price is what the story runs on.

The deepest of the three: **technology is culture.** A species' technology is not
a toolbox - it is their *inner life made physical*. You can tell what a people
love, fear, and believe by looking at what they know how to build. The game's
two civilizations - the Aria's humans and the Havari - are *proofs of this*: show
the player their tech, and the player understands their souls before a word is
spoken.

## 2. The Aria's humans - technology that is *earned, not grown*

Earth tech is brilliant, careful, and a little scared of itself. It is technology
of *separation*: machines that keep their distance from the body, instruments that
insulate. Humans learned the hard way (the wars, the long silence) and their
engineering has a wound in it: **every human device can be turned off, and every
human device keeps a record.**

### 2.1 The Aria itself - a ship that is a *contract*
- **Drive:** staged D-He3 fusion, ~5% c. The drive is a *generation-long engine* -
  its maintenance schedule is a heritage ritual; children inherit "their" reactor
  component the way Earth kids inherited names.
- **The hull is a garden:** everything is recyclable, everything is calibrated to
  last four generations. Nothing on the Aria is disposable - because out here,
  nothing can be.
- **The Log:** the ship keeps a complete, tamper-evident record of everything that
  ever happened on board. Voss respects the Log the way a priest respects a
  scripture. It is *honest*, and honesty is the Aria's religion.

### 2.2 Human medicine - repair as procedure
- **Cryo-to-palliative medicine:** not the "cure everything" of the aliens, but a
  deep, careful, *surgical* medicine. Human medtech is brilliant and conservative.
- **The BCI kit:** humans *have* brain-computer interfaces - fine, precise ones,
  descended from today's 65k-electrode chips and neural speech decoders. But they
  are **wearables, not implants**: headbands and throat-collars worn like
  spectacles, removable, audited, always with a physical kill-switch. Humans
  refused to let their minds be fused with anything they couldn't take off.
  (This is the human's tell: *fear of being unable to stop.*)
- **Translation software:** the Speaker's toolkit - real-time pitch contour
  analysis, phrase matching, the "ribbon" visualization. Human tech *translates
  the aliens* by drawing their meaning as a curve. It is a beautiful instrument
  and it is exactly wrong where it matters, because it treats meaning as data
  instead of as an offering. (See DESIGN_P3: translation is a moral act.)

### 2.3 What humans cannot do (and why it matters)
Humans cannot:
- change their shape (they can repair it, painfully, never *shape* it)
- speak mind-to-mind (they can decode, never converse)
- grow a tool (they must build it, and it stays built)
- un-remember anything the Log records (their past is a prison of accuracy)

Every one of these *inabilities* is a plot-shape waiting to be used. Voss's arc,
the Speaker's arc, and the theme of imperfection all *run on the fact that human
tech cannot make a human perfect.*

## 3. The Havari - technology that is *grown, not built*

> The Havari did not invent machines. They *domesticated* them - and then they
> domesticated the boundary between machine and self until it dissolved.

The Havari's technology is the 500-year endpoint of **Deblina Sarkar's** vision
("transcending humans beyond biological limitations") fused with **Michael
Levin's** bioelectric code. To the Havari, the difference between "body,"
"tool," and "city" is **a difference of scale, not of kind.** Everything is alive.
Everything listens. Everything is *them.*

### 3.1 The Weave - the Havari's second skin (from Sarkar)
- Every Havari carries, from birth, a **bioelectric lattice** woven through their
  tissues: subcellular nano-electronic threads, grown in, self-powered, silent.
  It is invisible. It is not worn; it *is* them.
- The Weave does what Sarkar's devices do today, at full bloom:
  - **Senses everything inside** - a complete, real-time map of the body, down to
    single cells. A Havari knows their own every heartbeat, every chemical drift.
  - **Repairs everything** - disease, injury, and age are *maintenance*. The
    Weave reads the body's intent and coaxes cells back to their target state.
    "Illness" is a thing the Havari have mostly forgotten - a child's-history
    word, like "famine."
  - **Thinks with the body** - the Weave *is* nervous tissue. A Havari's body is
    their computer, their phone, their instrument. Their *whole skin* hears
    (this is where the game's "they hear with their skin" biology comes from -
    it is *literal*).
- **The cost:** the Weave must be *fed*. It runs on resonance. A Havari at rest
  hums a fifth with itself (see DESIGN.md 2.1) not from habit but because **their
  own body's lattice needs a heartbeat of song to stay tuned.** The Weave is the
  reason song is survival to them. It is not a metaphor - it is their immune
  system, their clock, their memory. **Their culture's obsession with harmony is
  a medical fact.** This is the single most important sentence in the technology
  bible: *the Havari's perfectionism is not aesthetic; it is physiological.*
  When their culture forced itself into perfect consonance, it also silenced the
  Weave's own dissonant self-checks - and that is why their star-starving began.
  (This is the tech that *explains the plot without being the plot.*)

### 3.2 The Voice-net - technological telepathy (from bidirectional BCI)
- Havari communication is not *sound* in the human sense. The Weave lets every
  Havari **transmit and receive the substrate of thought itself** - a
  brain-to-brain channel that runs through the air as patterned resonance, the
  way today's EEG->TMS experiments pass a thought from one mind to another.
- Speaking to another Havari is **being felt.** A phrase is not "said" - it is
  *shared*, with texture: the speaker's mood, their certainty, their physical
  state, ride along inside the tone. This is why the Havari cannot lie: to emit
  a false statement through the Voice-net is to emit the *truth of your own
  state* alongside it, and the two contradict each other audibly. (This is the
  tech that makes the game's no-lies rule *physically real* - the Voice-net is
  not an honesty rule, it is an honesty *impossibility*.)
- **What the humans sound like to them:** a species whose minds are *sealed*,
  who can only broadcast an impoverished, filtered version of themselves through
  their mouths - and who can't even do that honestly. To the Havari, a human
  speaking is like watching a radio whose broadcast has been wiped of all but
  the carrier tone. It is why Thrael finds humans so hard to trust: *they sound
  like static.*

### 3.3 Morphing - total control of form (from Levin's bioelectric code)
- The Havari have an **anatomical compiler** (Levin's endgame): a command
  language for their own morphology. They can **rewrite their shape** - change
  how they move, how they look, how they sense - by editing the bioelectric
  prepattern their bodies regenerate toward.
- **What it looks like:** a Havari doesn't "gesture"; they *grow* a new fin for
  emphasis and dissolve it when done. A shy young Havari might make themselves
  smaller, a negotiator might *taller, more formal, more geometric* - and their
  chosen shape *is* their mood made visible. (This is the tech behind the game's
  per-character morphologies in DESIGN_P7 - Thrael's obelisk form is not
  costume, it is *posture at full power.*)
- **The cost - the game's heart:** morphing is effortless for them *except one
  thing*. The anatomical compiler can make a Havari taller, sharper, brighter -
  but it cannot make them **imperfect on purpose.** Their bodies always
  regenerate toward their *truest* shape, because that is what the compiler
  reads: the body's own target state. **A Havari literally cannot grow a flaw.**
  Which is why the player's broken, out-of-tune, gloriously imperfect voice is
  the one thing they can never copy. (See DESIGN.md: imperfection is the source
  of meaning - here, it is also the *only* thing their technology cannot do.)

### 3.4 The grown city (from xenobots / programmable matter)
- The Havari settlement on the outpost is **grown, not built**: structures that
  rose from seeded living matter, that heal their own cracks overnight, that
  *sense* who is inside them. A wall that remembers being leaned on. A path that
  dims for the person who prefers dark.
- **Everything is biodegradable and everything is self-repairing** (xenobots in
  full bloom). There is no waste because nothing is ever "thrown away" - a
  building that outlives its purpose is *sung back down* into soil, gratefully.
- The Havari find human prefab *fascinatingly strange*: dead matter, fixed
  shapes, no memory, no opinion. A human wall is, to them, a *very well-behaved
  corpse.* (Played for warmth, never cruelty.)

### 3.5 The cost of perfection - the single machine that hurts them
Every Havari gift has its mirror-bite:
- The **Weave** needs song to live -> perfect song *starves it*.
- The **Voice-net** cannot lie -> silence becomes their only lie, and the only
  place left to hide.
- The **compiler** cannot grow flaws -> they can never be *spontaneously,
  beautifully wrong*, and spontaneity is the thing their star is starving for.
- The **city** is grown -> when a Havari grieves, the building they're in
  grieves with them, and there is no neutral space to cry in.

**The summary of the whole bible:** *the Havari built technology that makes them
perfect - and their technology is a cage with the door on the inside, because
the lock is their own biology.* The player's technology can't do any of it - and
that is exactly why the player is the key.

## 4. Other species at the outpost (a few notes)

The outpost is crowded (DESIGN_P7). Other crews have their own technology-as-
culture. Three sketched so far - each is *one sentence of astonishment*:

- **The Vessk (radial, chitin-shelled):** their technology is *exoskeletal
  memory* - they record everything in the shells they shed, and carry their
  ancestors as armor. Their machines are the oldest living things on the outpost.
  (Astonishment: *their computers are their grandparents.*)
- **The Umlou (slow, soft, gravid):** their technology is *weather* - they
  navigate and communicate by cultivating local fields, pressure, and humidity
  into meaning. A Umlou conversation is a passing storm, gentle on purpose.
  (Astonishment: *they have never built anything; they have only ever asked the
  sky to remember for them.*)
- **The Chirr (small, fast, many-limbed):** their technology is *borrowed light*
  - they arrived with almost nothing and have been *reprogramming the outpost's
  own infrastructure* all season, to everyone's mixed delight and alarm. They
  are the outpost's unruly younger siblings and its best engineers.
  (Astonishment: *they can make your own lights dream in colors you didn't know
  existed - and they will, whether you asked or not.*)

These are seeds, not commitments. Each can grow into a friend, a foil, or a
background laugh as the story needs.

## 5. The two-technology meeting (the story's engine, kept out of the plot)

The entire game's tension is *two technologies colliding*:

| The Aria's humans | The Havari |
|---|---|
| Tech is **built**, separated from the body, auditable, kill-switchable | Tech is **grown**, fused with the body, permanent, no off-switch |
| Medicine **repairs** | Medicine **shapes** |
| Minds are **sealed**; you can only broadcast | Minds are **open**; you can only *share* |
| Honesty is a **choice** (the Log records what you did) | Honesty is a **physics** (the Voice-net records who you are) |
| Perfection is **impossible** (and they grieve it) | Perfection is **automatic** (and it is killing them) |
| The past is a **prison** of accurate record | The past is a **song** that can be re-sung |

Neither side is right. The story is not "humans win by being imperfect" - it is
**two peoples, each imprisoned by their own technology, meeting the only species
that holds the other's key.** The humans need the Havari's openness (Voss learns
to *be heard*). The Havari need the humans' brokenness (the Speaker's imperfect
voice can finally be *wrong on purpose*). They save each other by teaching each
other the use of the thing they lack.

And - the quietest, warmest truth of all: **a voice is the one technology both
species already share, and neither one has finished growing.**

## 6. Open questions (research + design)

1. **How much does the player know at start?** The Speaker is a human linguist -
   do they know the Weave's secret (harmony-as-health) at the beginning, or learn
   it alongside the player? (Design lean: they know the *science* but not the
   *consequence* - the reveal lands on them too.)
2. **Voss and the BCI kit:** does Voss wear the human headband-and-collar BCI? A
   nice beat: Voss, who "can't sing," is the one human most *fused* with his
   machine - a symmetry with the Havari he fears.
3. **The Log's limit:** if the Log records everything, what did it *fail* to
   record that matters? (The one honest gap that saves a life, or damns one.)
4. **Other species' tech:** which of the three sketched species earns a *scene*,
   and which stays background color? (Lean: Chirr get a scene - they're fun and
   they can be *the outpost's first hackers of a Havari-grown system*, which is
   a delightful near-miss.)
5. **Should the anatomical compiler be visible as an *effect* in-game** (a
   Havari visibly re-shaping mid-scene) as the visual proof of the bible?
