# RESONANCE - UNIVERSE: the world the stories live in

> "It is a small neighborhood. It is the whole universe we know, and it is
> enough. It is home."

## 1. What this is

RESONANCE is not one game with a plot. It is a **universe** - a utopian one -
built to hold many stories across many media: games, books, films, podcasts,
anything that needs a world with room to breathe.

This document is the **master frame**. It explains the shape of the universe and
where each piece of work fits. Every other design doc hangs off this one. If a
doc disagrees with this one, this one wins until it doesn't - and when it doesn't,
we rewrite this one too.

**The reframe:** the old docs (P2-P5) were written as "the game's plot and
mechanics." That was a narrowing. The universe is bigger than any one game:
- The **main game** is *open plot*: different individuals meeting and interacting.
  Goals are not locked yet - and may never be fully locked. It is a place to live
  in, and to be changed by.
- The **singing / Havari tonal theme** is a *mini-game theme* to be fleshed out
  separately - the game whose whole soul is the voice.
- The **worldbuilding** is the real product: a utopian future worth moving into,
  that books and films and podcasts could live in.

## 2. The utopian vision (the one rule)

This is a **utopian** universe. Not a perfect one - perfection is a cage, and
utopias that demand perfection are just dystopias with better lighting. It is a
universe where:

- **The big problems are solved.** War between peoples, famine, preventable
  disease, poverty: solved, or well on the way, because they were solved *before*
  the stories begin. Nobody in this universe is saving the world; the world is
  already worth living in.
- **The problems that remain are the good ones.** What do you do with a long
  life? How do you raise children who will never starve? What do you say to a
  stranger from another star when neither of you is afraid of the other? The
  dramas are dramas of *meaning*, not survival. This is what keeps it utopian
  without being dull: the stakes are love, honesty, home, purpose - the soft,
  hard things.
- **Kindness is infrastructure.** Goodwill is engineered into the systems -
  not because people are angels, but because they decided, generations ago, to
  build it so. The universe is warm. It can afford to be.

The tone rule, from the older docs, survives: **warm, light, often funny;
serious only when it needs to be.**

## 3. The geography (tiny on purpose)

The entire universe is two star systems, 4.2 light-years apart:

- **Sol / Earth** - where the Aria was built.
- **Proxima Centauri** - the flare star, home of the Havari, where the outpost is.

No FTL. No wormholes. No teleporters. (See DESIGN_P8 §1.5 - the two sins.) The
distances are real, so the stories are slow and warm instead of loud. A generation
ship is the only way to travel, and it takes ninety years to go next door.

This is the universe's signature: **everything of meaning happens inside one thin
neighborhood.** It keeps the stakes human-sized. It keeps the universe a *home*,
not a backdrop.

## 4. The peoples (so far)

### 4.1 The Aria's humans
A few thousand humans who left a post-scarcity Earth in the one slow way the
universe permits, and lived on the ship for generations. They are:
- **Direct and honest by culture** - they had no privacy, no luxury of pretending.
- **Practical, long-horizon, calm** - their habits were shaped by four generations
  in a tin can.
- **Ordinary.** The player is a gardener / engineer with a private, amateur singing
  habit. Not a chosen one. A neighbor.

See DESIGN_P6 (crew culture) and DESIGN_P8 §2 (their technology).

### 4.2 The Havari
Proxima's people. Their culture grew out of a tonal language - a voice-language
where feeling rides in every word, and so lying is *audible*. They are the oldest
residents of the newest town (DESIGN_P7). Not mystics, not perfect, not a "cage
with the door on the inside" - just a people whose lives were shaped by having a
language that says more than words. (See DESIGN.md §2 and DESIGN_P8 §3 - both
rewritten to remove the exoticized cliches.)

The singing / tonal theme is the seed of the **mini-game**, not the main game's
plot. The universe-wide theme is gentler and broader: voice, honesty, and the
small brave act of being imperfect on purpose.

### 4.3 The others
The outpost is crowded. Sketches exist for the Vessk (durable memory), the Umlou
(patient engineering), and the Chirr (borrowed light). See DESIGN_P8 §4. These are
seeds - they can grow into friends, foils, or background color in any story.

## 5. What the main game is (and isn't)

**It isn't:** a single locked plot with a hero, a villain, and a save-the-world
arc. Old P4-style plot beats are shelved, not sacred.

**It is:** a place where different individuals - human, Havari, others - meet and
interact. The player is one of them. The point is *who you meet and what happens
between you*, not what you were sent to do. Goals may emerge, or not. The design
question is open: what makes a meeting meaningful, and what does the player *do*
that's satisfying, if the goal is not "win"?

Early candidates for "what the player does" (not decided, just seeds):
- **Talk.** Conversation is the core mechanic; the game is a dialogue sandbox with
  all the craft laws of the old docs behind it.
- **Listen.** The player is the one person who actually hears the Havari - the
  translation game, reading emotion as contour.
- **Care for things.** Gardening, maintenance, fixing - the small labors that build
  trust in a community. (The player is a gardener/engineer for a reason.)
- **Sing, privately.** A private, imperfect habit that has nothing to do with
  saving anyone - and might matter to someone anyway.

None of these is locked. The main game is a question mark on purpose, and it is a
happy one.

## 6. The mini-game (the singing theme)

Separate from the main game: the piece where the Havari's tonal language and the
player's broken voice are the whole point. Fleshed out later. It inherits the
mechanics seeded in DESIGN.md §5 and the craft guides in DESIGN_P5 - the
pitch-game, the emotion-as-contour reading, the no-lies rule. It is where the
"imperfection is the source of meaning" theme gets its fullest expression, because
it can *play* the voice.

## 7. Media in the universe (books, films, podcasts)

Because the universe is the product, stories in other media should be able to:
- Follow any character from any doc as their own protagonist.
- Visit Earth, the Aria, Proxima, or the outpost - or never leave one room.
- Be told from any species' point of view.
- Have any tone the universe allows - as long as it is warm at the core and
  serious only when needed.

The docs are written so any given character (Voss, Nnamdi, Osen, the Speaker,
Seravak, Ilyan, Thrael, the Pulse, the Chirr) is a person with a full life
outside the main game. Nothing in the universe exists *only* to serve a plot.

## 9. Open questions (for the universe)

1. **The main game's "do":** what is the satisfying core activity? (Talk/listen/
   care/sing - or something else?) The old docs have craft laws but no locked loop.
2. **How much structure?** Open sandbox, vignette anthology, or something between?
   (Lean: vignette anthology - scenes of meeting - is the most "universe-like" and
   the best fit for books/podcasts to share DNA with.)
3. **The mini-game's scope:** a pitch-matching puzzle, an emotion-reading game, a
   story about the broken voice - or a small game that is all three?
4. **Which older docs get reframed first?** P2/P3/P4 contain good material inside
   one-game framing. Recast as universe lore rather than deleted.
5. **Naming:** is "RESONANCE" the universe name, or the main game's name? (Does the
   universe need a name that isn't also a game's title?)
