# RESONANCE — Design Bible

> The core of this universe is story and character. Graphics, music, animation, and systems exist
> only to heighten the emotional and intellectual experience of *meeting beings who are not
> human*. Every alien is written to *surprise* you — to break your expectations of what a
> mind, a heart, and a voice can be.

> Frame: this is a **utopian universe** (UNIVERSE.md). The main game has an open
> plot — different individuals meeting and interacting. The singing / Havari tonal
> theme is the **mini-game** theme. This file is the thematic bible both share.

---

## 1. Thematic Core

**Imperfection is the source of meaning.** Every character, every mechanic, every beat
circles this one idea. The Havari are the most perfect singers of their world — a
civilization that raised flawless performance into a way of life, and that perfection is
slowly smothering them. And the only voice that can help them is one that wobbles — a
human voice, broken, uncertain, and feeling-soaked. This is the heart of the **mini-game**,
and a theme that runs everywhere in the universe, not a locked main-game plot.

Secondary themes woven throughout:
- **Translation is an act of love, not of accuracy.** The "right" translation is not the
  literal one; it's the one that builds trust.
- **What you cannot say, you reveal.** The Havari cannot lie — so their honesty is also a
  cage, and learning to *withhold* (dissonance as silence) becomes their great moral question.
- **Listening is a skill. Hearing is a gift.**

## 2. The Havari — Alien Psychology First

The Havari are NOT "humans who sing." They are a species whose inner life grew out of
tone — but tone as *language*, not as magic. Every assumption they hold follows from
having a language that says more than words, and each one is *norm-breaking in human
eyes*. The point is not that they are inscrutable; it is that they are *coherent*. Given
a voice-language, everything else is inevitable, the way human assumptions follow from
having hands and faces. Once the player understands the language, the Havari stop being
strange and start being *people*.

### 2.1 Biology → Cognition

- Havari are motile, bioluminescent chordate-floaters: a main trunk with a mantle of
  resonant bladders. They are sensitive to vibration across their whole body surface —
  an array of pressure-receptors spread over the skin, the way our touch is spread over
  our hands. This is real and fine, but it is *a sense*, not a soul: it means they feel
  a rumble through the floor when you walk, and a soft voice reaches them faintly the
  way a whisper reaches us across a room. Being unheard is *isolation*, not agony.
- They produce sound via a set of **independent laryngeal tubes** (3-7). They can sing
  **chords to themselves** — a Havari is always, acoustically, at least a duo. Solitude
  (singing alone) feels like **poverty of voice** — a Havari at rest hums a fifth with
  itself the way a human at rest hums a tune. It is a habit, not a law.
- Because their voices carry so much of who they are, **identity is a voiceprint.** The
  self is not a timbre-mystery; it is simply that their word for "I" is inseparable
  from "how I sound," the way ours is inseparable from "this body." You recognize a
  Havari the way you recognize a friend's laugh from across a room.

### 2.2 The Unspoken Assumptions (each is a *surprise* for the player)

1. **They have no comfortable word for "lie."** They have a word for what a lie *is*:
   *"to sing against your own voice"* — to make your tone say one thing while your
   feeling says another. Because their language is tonal, **the mismatch is audible**:
   a Havari who tries to deceive produces a wobble, a "blur," the way a human's voice
   rises and wavers when they lie on the phone. So:
   - They are **bad at lying** — not because they are holy, but because their
     instrument is transparent. Their voice tattles on them.
   - This does NOT make them saints. It makes them **unable to fake virtue** — they
     cannot perform politeness they don't feel. Their cruelty is naked and shocking.
   - **The moral dilemma of the game:** the only "lie" they have is *silence* — to stop
     singing, to withdraw. Learning to *choose silence* becomes a mature, difficult act.

2. **Death is not the worst thing. Unfinished business is.** To die in tune with the
   world is a good death ("a cadence") — a song finished properly. To die *wrongly* —
   off-key, unresolved, abandoned mid-phrase — is a grief that never resolves, like a
   sentence left hanging forever. The star's slowing pulse is feared not as "death" but
   as **the song everyone is part of going out of tune.** It is their framework for
   dread, not a cosmic law.

3. **Memory is sung, not written.** The Havari built a culture with almost no writing;
   sound was their page. A Havari "remembers" by re-singing. Their histories are
   *performances*, kept alive by specialists the way we keep a library — but a library
   that must be performed to exist. Retellings drift, like an oral epic handed down;
   their history is partly legend and they *know* it, and mostly they are at peace with
   it. Their long records are not choirs that sing the past by magic — they are
   *trained voices doing a job*, like any living oral tradition, at institutional scale.

4. **Time is a rhythm, not a calendar.** Proxima is a flare star; its light surges and
   calms on no fixed schedule, and Havari time is anchored to that pulse. "Tomorrow" is
   "the next downbeat." Long-term planning is weak; their great projects are *musical
   forms* (movements), not goals. The human idea of "a deadline" is bewildering to them
   — they ask: *"why does the melody have to end on a downbeat?"*

5. **Emotion is contour.** Joy = rising, bright. Sorrow = descending, weighted. Longing =
   a sustained high note that wants to fall. Fear = tritone (the "unstable interval").
   The player LEARNS this vocabulary — and can read alien mood directly from their
   melodies, which is a literal gameplay mechanic (see §5).

6. **Children are out of tune by nature.** Havari young are born unable to sing in tune
   and *grow into* it, the way human children are born unable to talk. So
   **immaturity = dissonance, maturity = consonance.** A "child" is a being who is
   honestly off-key. This reverses human intuition: when an adult Havari sings a
   "child's" imperfect phrase, it is either profound humility — or a profound insult.
   (This is what Thrael's plot hinges on.)

### 2.3 The Two Taboos

Two things Havari will do almost anything to avoid:
- **Singing alone in the void** (total isolation of voice).
- **Sustaining an unresolved dissonance** (a suspended, agonizing interval that never
  resolves — what a human would call "writer's block of the soul" but for them it is
  torture).
