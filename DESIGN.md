# RESONANCE — Design Bible

> The core of this game is story and character. Graphics, music, animation, and systems exist
> only to heighten the emotional and intellectual experience of *meeting beings who are not
> human*. Every alien is written to *surprise* you — to break your expectations of what a
> mind, a heart, and a voice can be.

---

## 1. Thematic Core

**Imperfection is the source of meaning.** Every character, every mechanic, every beat
circles this one idea. The Havari are the most perfect singers in the galaxy — and that
perfection is slowly killing them. The human hero, whose singing is broken, uncertain, and
feeling-soaked, turns out to be the only voice that can save them.

Secondary themes woven throughout:
- **Translation is an act of love, not of accuracy.** The "right" translation is not the
  literal one; it's the one that builds trust.
- **What you cannot say, you reveal.** The Havari cannot lie — so their honesty is also a
  cage, and learning to *withhold* (dissonance as silence) becomes their great moral question.
- **Listening is a skill. Hearing is a gift.**

---

## 2. The Havari — Alien Psychology First

The Havari are NOT "humans who sing." They are a species whose entire inner life grew out
of tone. This shapes every assumption they hold, and every one of those assumptions will
be *norm-breaking in human eyes*.

### 2.1 Biology → Cognition

- Havari are motile, bioluminescent chordate-floaters (a "choir body": a main trunk with a
  mantle of resonant bladders). They hear not with organs but with their **whole skin**.
  Every cell resonates. This is why their language is total-body: to speak is to be heard
  in your bones. To them, a *whisper* is a physically weaker vibration — being unheard is
  a near-painful isolation.
- They produce sound via a set of **independent laryngeal tubes** (3-7). They can sing
  **chords to themselves** — a Havari is always, acoustically, at least a duo. Solitude
  (singing alone) is literally **poverty of voice** — a Havari at rest hums a fifth with
  itself.
- Because their harmonics ARE their self, **the concept of "solo" barely exists.** The
  self is a *timbre*, a quality — the "I" is what makes your partials glisten differently
  from everyone else's. Individual identity = overtone signature.

### 2.2 The Unspoken Assumptions (each is a *surprise* for the player)

1. **They have no word for "lie."** But they have a word for what a lie does: *"to sing
   against your own overtone signature"* — a dissonance that makes the speaker *audibly
   ugly* to other Havari. Falsehood is physically painful to hear. So:
   - They **cannot lie fluently**. A Havari attempting deception produces an audible
     "blur," like a cracked chord.
   - This does NOT make them saints. It makes them *unable to fake virtue* — they cannot
     perform politeness they don't feel. Their cruelty is naked and shocking.
   - **The moral dilemma of the game:** the only "lie" they have is *silence* — to stop
     singing, to withdraw. Learning to *choose silence* becomes a mature, difficult act.

2. **Death is not the worst thing. Dis-harmony is.** To die in tune with the world is a
   good death ("a cadence"). To die *wrongly* — off-key, unresolved, abandoned mid-phrase —
   is a cosmic horror. The star's slowing pulse is not feared as "death"; it's feared as
   **the world going out of tune forever.**

3. **Memory is melodic.** A Havari "remembers" a thing by re-singing it. Historical
   records are *performances*. To *misremember* is to sing a different tune. This is why
   their history is partly legend: each retelling is a genuine act of creation, and no two
   tellings are identical. **Their archives are literally choirs that sing the past.**

4. **Time is a rhythm, not a line.** The Havari think in cyclic time (the pulse of their
   star). "Tomorrow" is "the next downbeat." Long-term planning is weak; their great
   projects are *musical forms* (movements), not goals. The human idea of "a deadline" is
   bewildering to them — they ask: *"why does the melody have to end on a downbeat?"*

5. **Emotion is contour.** Joy = rising, bright. Sorrow = descending, weighted. Longing =
   a sustained high note that wants to fall. Fear = tritone (the "unstable interval").
   The player LEARNS this vocabulary — and can read alien mood directly from their
   melodies, which is a literal gameplay mechanic (see §5).

6. **Children are dissonant by design.** Havari young are born unable to sing in tune and
   *grow into* harmony. So **immaturity = dissonance, maturity = consonance.** A "child" is
   a being who is honestly out of tune. This reverses human intuition: when an adult Havari
   sings a "child's" imperfect phrase, it is either a profound humility — or a profound
   insult. (This is what Thrael's plot hinges on.)

### 2.3 The Two Taboos

Two things Havari will do almost anything to avoid:
- **Singing alone in the void** (total isolation of voice).
- **Sustaining an unresolved dissonance** (a suspended, agonizing interval that never
  resolves — what a human would call "writer's block of the soul" but for them it is
  torture).
