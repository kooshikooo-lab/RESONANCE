# RESONANCE — Design Bible (Part 2: Grammar, Characters)

---

## 3. The Tonal Grammar (the "language" the player actually uses)

The player doesn't learn alien phonemes. They learn a *musical grammar*. Over the game,
the player accumulates **Phrases** (melodic words) and **Forms** (how to string them).

- **A Phrase** = 3-6 notes with a contour (e.g., "greeting = rising fifth + returning
  home"). Each has a *meaning* that is not arbitrary: it sounds like what it means.
- **A Form** = an emotional/intentional frame (question = phrase ends on rising leading
  tone; statement = cadence down; request = suspense + resolve; refusal = tritone slide).
- The player sings Echoes (reproduce a phrase exactly) and Dialogues (choose the *right
  response* from their learned set, judged on grammar, not rote).

**Reading alien mood is real:** because emotion is contour, the player can look at a
melody's shape on screen and *know* whether Seravak is testing, grieving, or flirting.
This is the game's way of making "empathy with an alien" a mechanic, not a cutscene.

## 4. Characters

> Every character has depth AND a norm-breaking core. None is a "human in a costume."
> The player should be *genuinely surprised* by each of them, usually more than once.

### 4.1 THE SPEAKER (player) — "you sing because you can't say it"

**Human. A linguist-musician** on a first-contact vessel. Trauma: the Speaker's whole
profession is built on the belief that *meaning must be exact* — and exactness once cost
them everything (a mis-translation, a misunderstanding, a crew lost in the silence of
space). They are now **afraid of their own voice**: afraid of being heard, of being
*responsible for a meaning*. The game's arc: the Speaker learns that **imperfect sound is
the only honest sound**, that meaning survives being mangled, that trust is built by
offering your voice even when you'll be wrong.

**Player agency:** the player's actual singing accuracy IS the character's confidence.
When the player sings well, the Speaker becomes braver; the script's dialogue reflects
the player's skill level (voice lines differ at high/low accuracy). The game *feels* the
player's fear and rewards their courage.

### 4.2 SERAVAK — the Teacher (sage-green, lowest voice) ★ THE FIRST SURPRISE

**Surface:** the patient elder; the wise guide; the one who greets you gently. The player
expects a Yoda.

**The surprise:** Seravak is NOT kind. Seravak is a **connoisseur of imperfection.** She
teaches you not from charity but from *fascination*: your broken, human, out-of-tune
singing is the most interesting thing she has heard in a thousand years. She is delighted
by your *mistakes* — she collects them. She will deliberately teach you a wrong phrase to
hear what you do with it. She is, in human terms, **an artist using you as material.**

**Deeper still:** her love of imperfection is grief in disguise. Seravak is the last of
her *line* — a singer whose entire sub-song (family chord) has died out. She has been
singing alone-in-chords for centuries. Teaching you is the first time in centuries she
sings *with* someone. Her "artistic detachment" cracks as she realizes you are not a
specimen but a **second voice**. Her gift to the player (late game): she reveals the
*humility phrase* — the "child's phrase" — and tells you it was hers, sung wrong on
purpose, to keep her dead family "in the room."

**Norm-breaking core:** *she loves you for your flaws, which is both deeply alien and,
turned around, the most human thing anyone has ever done for you.*

### 4.3 ILYAN — the Young (gold voice, bright, slightly sharp) ★ THE SECOND SURPRISE

**Surface:** the playful child; the adorable mascot; the one who teaches you songs and
laughs at your mistakes. The player expects a cute alien sidekick.

**The surprise:** Ilyan is the **revolutionary.** Young Havari are dissonant — and Ilyan
*refuses to grow into consonance.* Ilyan is the leader of a quiet youth movement that
believes the Havari have perfected themselves into **sterility** — that harmony has become
a prison, that the star is dying *because* the culture stopped taking risks. Ilyan
deliberately sings *wrong* as a political act. Ilyan befriends you not out of innocence
but because **you are living proof that dissonance can be beautiful and true.** You are
Ilyan's argument.

**Deeper still:** Ilyan is terrified. The "child who won't grow up" is the one who most
fears the *other* taboo — Ilyan is secretly the only young Havari who has sung the
*unresolved dissonance* — and it nearly destroyed them. Their rebellion is a mask for
their terror of their own sensitivity. They are the character who most needs the player
to *listen without judging.*

**Norm-breaking core:** *the child is the most politically sophisticated, most
existentially brave, and most fragile being in the game — and its "immaturity" is a
chosen, principled stance.*
